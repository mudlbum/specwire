**SpecWire** exists because technology specifications are unusually easy to publish and unusually hard to check.

Almost nothing on a spec sheet is regulated. "1ms response time" has no legally defined measurement condition. "HDR" on its own certifies nothing. "All-day battery" describes a test loop you were never shown. "120 W fast charging" is a peak the device holds for a fraction of the charge. "AI TOPS" is a number two vendors can calculate differently and still both be telling the truth. None of this is fraud, exactly — it is what happens when an industry markets against figures no independent body is required to verify.

A minority of those numbers *are* governed by real, published, testable standards. VESA's DisplayHDR and ClearMR programmes have compliance test specifications you can download and read. HDMI and DisplayPort bandwidth is arithmetic. USB-IF, Bluetooth SIG and JEDEC publish what their logos oblige. IP ratings specify exactly which test a device passed.

The gap between those two categories — claims that mean something and claims that do not — is what this site is about.

## What we do

We read the documents. Compliance test specifications, silicon and panel datasheets, manufacturer specification pages in their original wording, published model cards, standards-body feature tables. Then we set them against what the marketing says and report the difference, with the revision and the date each figure was current.

Where we cover a product, we link to the manufacturer's own specification page so you can check our transcription rather than trust it.

Where a claim can only be settled by measurement, we cite the labs that measure — by name, with a link, identifying what equipment and methodology produced the number.

## What we do not do

**We do not test hardware.** No lab, no colorimeter rig, no battery bench, no anechoic chamber, no benchmark suite of our own. Any measured value on this site came from somebody else's instruments and is attributed to them by name, in the sentence, with a link.

We say this plainly because the alternative is standard practice in this category: sites that imply testing they never did, republish other outlets' numbers without attribution, and photograph a product on a desk to suggest a review unit. If you want measurements, go to the people who take them — [RTINGS](https://www.rtings.com/), [TFTCentral](https://tftcentral.co.uk/), [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/), [Monitors Unboxed](https://www.youtube.com/@monitorsunboxed). They are excellent, and we link to them constantly.

What they mostly do not do — because it is not their job — is explain the standards underneath the numbers, or track what a certification badge actually obliges a manufacturer to deliver. That is the job we took.

**We do not publish rankings we cannot defend.** No "best phone 2026" list assembled from spec sheets. If we recommend, we say what the recommendation rests on.

**We do not accept payment for coverage.** No sponsored posts, no paid placement, no review units in exchange for favourable treatment.

**We do not use manufacturer press images or other outlets' photography.** We have no licence to them. Artwork here is generated from the article's own data, or licensed stock, and official product pages get linked rather than scraped.

## What we cover

- **[Phones](/phones/)** — chipsets and sustained performance, camera sensors and binning, charging wattage versus real charge time, battery and durability ratings.
- **[AI](/ai/)** — context windows, benchmark methodology, NPU ratings, on-device versus cloud inference, and how to read a model card.
- **[Displays & TVs](/displays/)** — DisplayHDR tiers in cd/m², panel structure, response time and motion clarity.
- **[Explainers](/explainers/)** — one unit at a time: nits, TOPS, Gbps, mAh, process nodes. What each measures and how each is gamed.

Two further sections open as their first articles publish: **Laptops & PCs** (CPU and GPU tiers, what a TDP figure promises against what the thermals allow, memory and port bandwidth) and **Audio & Wearables** (codecs and their real bitrates, how ANC is measured, latency, and what a battery-life claim assumes). A section with no articles in it is a thin page, so it stays invisible until it has something to say.

## Review round-ups

Some articles summarise what the outlets that actually tested a product found. When we do that, the article says so at the top, every measured number names the outlet that produced it in the same sentence, and we link out so you can read the original. We do not reproduce another outlet's prose, tables or photography, and a round-up has to carry substantial original analysis — where the testers disagree, what the spec sheet claimed versus what was measured — or it does not run.

## Who writes it

Articles are researched, drafted and published automatically using AI tooling that reads the standards documents directly, and each one must clear an automated sourcing gate before it can go live — no resolving source for a figure means no publication. A human reviews articles after they publish rather than before, and corrections are logged and dated in public. Our [editorial and AI policy](/editorial-policy/) sets out exactly how that works and what the tooling may not do; the [corrections policy](/corrections/) explains what happens when we get something wrong.

We do not invent bylines, we do not fabricate expert quotes, and we do not manufacture personal anecdotes about testing we did not perform.

## How the site is funded

Display advertising, and in future possibly affiliate links — which will be labelled as such wherever they appear. Advertisers and affiliate programmes have no input into what we cover or what we conclude, and nobody sees an article before it is published. Where a piece discusses a product we could earn a commission on, that is disclosed on the page.
